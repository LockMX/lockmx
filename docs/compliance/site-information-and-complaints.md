## 1. Identification and contacts (DL 7/2004, art. 10, n.º 1)

The provider must make available permanently online, in conditions that allow easy and direct access, complete identification elements:

- a) name or company name;
- b) geographic address where established, and email address;
- c) registrations in public registers and their numbers;
- d) tax identification number.

The following paragraphs cover prior authorization (n.º 2), regulated professions (n.º 3) and a n.º 4 that requires clear information before use of the services; the wording of n.º 4 was not read in full. The shop sells goods and no regulated profession, so n.º 2 and n.º 3 are not expected to apply. To confirm.

Penalty (art. 37.º): fine from 2,500 to 50,000 euros for failing arts. 10.º, 21.º and 28.º n.º 1 a); raised by one third for legal persons (n.º 6).

Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=1399&tabela=leis (2026-10-08). The page lists the versions up to Lei n.º 12-A/2026, of 15 April, and no amendment note on art. 10.º.

Consequence for the site: a permanent, one-click page (footer link) with company name, registered address, `support@lockmx.com`, commercial registry data and tax number. The values are supplied by the client and are not invented.

## 2. Information before the order (DL 7/2004, art. 28 n.º 1)

The provider must give recipients, before the order is placed, minimal unambiguous information. Only the opening of the article was read. The list of items is to be read in the full article before the checkout spec.

## 3 and 4. Distance contracts (DL 24/2014, art. 4)

- Art. 4.º is the pre-contract information duty for distance contracts. DL 78/2018 rewrote it. In the new alínea c) of n.º 1 the physical address of the professional's establishment must be given, and "where the consumer can present a complaint" (source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2958&tabela=leis&ficha=1&pagina=1&so_miolo=S, 2026-10-08).
- The consumer association APCMC states that the amendment made the telephone number and email address mandatory where before they had to be given only if they existed (https://apcmc.pt/legislacao/defesa-dos-consumidores-vendas-a-distancia-ou-fora-do-estabelecimento/, 2026-10-08). The wording of the alínea was not retrieved from an official text. **Open:** confirm in the Diário da República before launch. Until then the plan assumes the business must show a telephone number and a physical address.
- Art. 10.º: the withdrawal period is 14 days; it becomes 12 months if the information required by art. 4.º n.º 1 alínea m) (withdrawal right and form) is not given (https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2062&tabela=leis, 2026-10-08). The terms of sale and the checkout must carry that information.
- Art. 4.º-A applies to online marketplaces and does not apply to a shop selling its own products.

## 5 and 6. Complaints Book (DL 156/2005, as amended by DL 74/2017, DL 9/2021, DL 103/2025)

Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=737&tabela=leis (2026-10-08; the page lists versions up to DL 102/2026, of 22 May).

- Art. 2.º, n.º 2: the electronic format covers providers who trade through digital means.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 1: the supplier must have the electronic format of the book.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 2: the supplier must publish, on its website, access to the Digital Platform "in a visible place and in a prominent way". The platform is at `www.livroreclamacoes.pt`.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 3: only suppliers without a website need an email address to receive complaints.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 4: the supplier answers the consumer within a maximum of 15 working days.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 6: having the electronic format does not remove the obligation to have the physical book where it applies.
- Art. 3.º, n.º 1 a): the physical book is kept "at the establishments to which the activity relates". **Open:** whether an online-only business with no establishment open to the public needs the physical book. To confirm with the sector authority (ASAE) or a legal adviser.
- Art. 5.º, n.º 3: the supplier may offer its own problem-solving tools on the website, provided they are clearly distinguished from the Complaints Book. Third-party platforms such as Portal da Queixa are private and are not the official book.
- Art. 9.º-A: for breaches of art. 5.º-B n.º 1 to 3 a warning notice with a 90-day period comes before any fine. Fine amounts follow the general economic offences regime (RJCE), not read.
- **Open:** the registration of the operator in the platform. A registration page exists at https://www.livroreclamacoes.pt/Operador/registooperadores; the procedure was not read.

Consequence for the site: a visible, prominent link to the Complaints Book platform in the footer (and in the checkout), on every page, in PT and EN.

## 7. Consumer dispute resolution entities (Lei 144/2015, art. 18)

- Suppliers established in Portugal must inform consumers of the dispute resolution entities they are bound to (by adhesion or by mandatory arbitration), with their websites.
- The information must be clear, understandable and easily accessible, on the website and in written contracts or adhesion contracts.
- Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2425&tabela=leis&so_miolo=S (2026-10-08). Amended by DL 102/2017.
- **Open:** whether the client adheres to a consumer arbitration centre, and what the law requires from a business that is not bound to any. Decide with the client; the terms of sale and the footer state the answer.

## 8. EU online dispute resolution platform

The European ODR platform was discontinued. Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 repeals Regulation (EU) 524/2013 with effect from 20 July 2025, and the Commission closed the platform; online sellers no longer have to link to it. Many templates still do, and the site must not.

Sources: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3228/oj/eng and https://consumer-redress.ec.europa.eu/site-relocation_en (2026-10-08).

## What the site needs from the client

Before the marketing and legal spec starts, the client provides:

- company name, registered address, commercial registry number, tax number;
- a telephone number and a physical address where complaints can be made;
- whether the client adheres to a consumer arbitration centre;
- the decision on the physical Complaints Book.
