## 1. Identification and contacts (DL 7/2004, art. 10, n.º 1)

The provider must make available permanently online, in conditions that allow easy and direct access, complete identification elements:

- a) name or company name;
- b) geographic address where established, and email address;
- c) registrations in public registers and their numbers;
- d) tax identification number.

The following paragraphs cover prior authorization (n.º 2), regulated professions (n.º 3) and a n.º 4 that requires clear information before use of the services; the wording of n.º 4 was not read in full. The shop sells goods and no regulated profession, so n.º 2 and n.º 3 are not expected to apply. To confirm.

Penalty (art. 37.º): fine from 2,500 to 50,000 euros for failing arts. 10.º, 21.º and 28.º n.º 1 a); raised by one third for legal persons (n.º 6).

Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=1399&tabela=leis (2026-10-08). The page lists the versions up to Lei n.º 12-A/2026, of 15 April, and no amendment note on art. 10.º.

Consequence for the site: a permanent, one-click page (footer link) with company name, registered address, `support@lockmx.com`, commercial registry data and tax number. The values are supplied by the client and are not invented.

## 2. Information before the order (DL 7/2004, art. 28 n.º 1)

The provider must give recipients, before the order is placed, minimal unambiguous information. Only the opening of the article was read. The list of items is to be read in the full article before the checkout spec.

## 3 and 4. Distance contracts (DL 24/2014, art. 4)

- Art. 4.º is the pre-contract information duty for distance contracts. DL 78/2018 rewrote it. In the new alínea c) of n.º 1 the physical address of the professional's establishment must be given, and "where the consumer can present a complaint" (source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2958&tabela=leis&ficha=1&pagina=1&so_miolo=S, 2026-10-08).
- The consumer association APCMC states that the amendment made the telephone number and email address mandatory where before they had to be given only if they existed (https://apcmc.pt/legislacao/defesa-dos-consumidores-vendas-a-distancia-ou-fora-do-estabelecimento/, 2026-10-08).
- Read on 2026-10-14 in the consolidated text (https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2062&tabela=leis; last amendment listed: Lei n.º 10/2023, of 3 March). Art. 4.º n.º 1 a): the identity of the supplier, "incluindo o nome, a firma ou denominação social, o endereço físico onde se encontra estabelecido, o número de telefone e o endereço eletrónico". The consolidated text is not the Diário da República, which was not read; nothing found contradicts it.
- **Consequence:** the site shows a telephone number, the physical address and the email address before the order, on the company page and on `/contact`. The telephone number and the address come from the client.
- Art. 10.º: the withdrawal period is 14 days; it becomes 12 months if the information required by art. 4.º n.º 1 alínea m) (withdrawal right and form) is not given (https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2062&tabela=leis, 2026-10-08). The terms of sale and the checkout must carry that information.
- Art. 4.º-A applies to online marketplaces and does not apply to a shop selling its own products.

## 5 and 6. Complaints Book (DL 156/2005, as amended by DL 74/2017, DL 9/2021, DL 103/2025)

Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=737&tabela=leis (2026-10-08; the page lists versions up to DL 102/2026, of 22 May).

- Art. 2.º, n.º 2: the electronic format covers providers who trade through digital means.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 1: the supplier must have the electronic format of the book.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 2: the supplier must publish, on its website, access to the Digital Platform "in a visible place and in a prominent way". The platform is at `www.livroreclamacoes.pt`.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 3: only suppliers without a website need an email address to receive complaints.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 4: the supplier answers the consumer within a maximum of 15 working days.
- Art. 5.º-B, n.º 6: having the electronic format does not remove the obligation to have the physical book where it applies.
- Art. 3.º, n.º 1 a): the physical book is kept "at the establishments to which the activity relates". **Open:** whether an online-only business with no establishment open to the public needs the physical book. To confirm with the sector authority (ASAE) or a legal adviser.
- Art. 5.º, n.º 3: the supplier may offer its own problem-solving tools on the website, provided they are clearly distinguished from the Complaints Book. Third-party platforms such as Portal da Queixa are private and are not the official book.
- Art. 9.º-A: for breaches of art. 5.º-B n.º 1 to 3 a warning notice with a 90-day period comes before any fine. Fine amounts follow the general economic offences regime (RJCE), not read.
- **Open:** the registration of the operator in the platform. A registration page exists at https://www.livroreclamacoes.pt/Operador/registooperadores; the procedure was not read.
- **How to show the access (read 2026-10-14).** A note of the Direção-Geral do Consumidor, "Livro de Reclamações Eletrónico: Obrigações do prestador de serviços ou fornecedor de bens", undated and hosted by the transport regulator AMT (https://www.amt-autoridade.pt/media/2053/manual_utilizacao_icone_lre.pdf); the page of the DGC itself was not read. It says:
  - the operator must be registered on the platform and answer within 15 working days;
  - the law only requires disclosing "o acesso à plataforma do livro de reclamações eletrónico", with no set form;
  - in the DGC's understanding the access should "preferencialmente" be the icon of the Complaints Book, in a visible and prominent place, linked to the platform;
  - minimum criteria for the icon: red, blue, white or black; it must contrast with the background; it links to `https://www.livroreclamacoes.pt/inicio`; it appears on the home page (header or footer), and also on the customer support page as good practice; an internal complaints channel of the business must be clearly told apart from the Complaints Book.
  - The icon files (its "anexo 1") were not found. The footer today has a text link to `https://www.livroreclamacoes.pt`, which meets the law as read; the icon is the DGC's preference. Using it is a new asset on a public page, so it is the owner's choice, and the files come from an official source.

Consequence for the site: a visible, prominent link to the Complaints Book platform in the footer (and in the checkout), on every page, in PT and EN.

## 7. Consumer dispute resolution entities (Lei 144/2015, art. 18)

- Suppliers established in Portugal must inform consumers of the dispute resolution entities they are bound to (by adhesion or by mandatory arbitration), with their websites.
- The information must be clear, understandable and easily accessible, on the website and in written contracts or adhesion contracts.
- Source: https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=2425&tabela=leis&so_miolo=S (2026-10-08). Amended by DL 102/2017.
- Art. 18.º n.º 1 as amended by DL 102/2017 (read 2026-10-14 in the consolidated text): the duty covers the entities the business is bound to "por adesão ou por imposição legal decorrente de arbitragem necessária".
- Lei 24/96, art. 14.º n.º 2 and 3, as amended by Lei 63/2019 (read 2026-10-14, https://www.pgdlisboa.pt/leis/lei_mostra_articulado.php?nid=726&tabela=leis): consumer disputes up to the alçada of the courts of first instance are subject to "arbitragem necessária ou mediação" when the consumer chooses to take them to a legally authorised consumer arbitration centre. The alçada was not read; secondary sources give 5,000 euros.
- **Consequence (our reading, not legal advice):** every business is bound by law to the consumer arbitration centres for those disputes, at the consumer's choice, so the information is due even without adhering to any centre. A director of a sector centre said the same to the press in 2019 (Lusa, via https://www.sabado.pt/dinheiro/detalhe/empresas-sujeitas-a-partir-de-hoje-a-arbitragem-em-litigios-de-consumo-ate-5000-euros). Adhering (for example to CNIACC, "adesão plena", https://www.cniacc.pt/pt/o-que-e-adesao-plena-de-empresas-ao-cniacc) is voluntary.
- **Which centre:** a regional centre is competent when the consumer's home or the place of the contract is in its area; CNIACC covers the areas no regional centre covers (CNIACC home page, read 2026-10-14). A DGC brochure, quoted second hand, says that for online sales the centre of the consumer's home is competent. For a shop selling to the whole country, any centre may be competent, so the site lists them all.
- **The official list** (DGC, "Lista das Entidades de Resolução Alternativa de Litígios de Consumo", https://www.consumidor.gov.pt/upload/processos/i006589.pdf, undated, read 2026-10-14). Generic competence:
  - CNIACC, Centro Nacional de Informação e Arbitragem de Conflitos de Consumo: national, where no regional centre covers; https://www.cniacc.pt/pt/
  - CIMAAL, Centro de Informação, Mediação e Arbitragem de Conflitos de Consumo do Algarve: district of Faro; www.consumidoronline.pt
  - Centro de Arbitragem de Conflitos de Consumo do Distrito de Coimbra: www.centrodearbitragemdecoimbra.com
  - Centro de Arbitragem de Conflitos de Consumo de Lisboa: Lisbon metropolitan area; www.centroarbitragemlisboa.pt
  - CICAP, Centro de Informação de Consumo e Arbitragem do Porto: Porto metropolitan area; www.cicap.pt
  - TRIAVE, Centro de Arbitragem de Conflitos de Consumo do Vale do Ave / Tribunal Arbitral: www.triave.pt
  - CIAB, Centro de Informação, Mediação e Arbitragem de Consumo (Tribunal Arbitral de consumo): Braga and Viana do Castelo areas; www.ciab.pt
  - Centro de Arbitragem de Conflitos de Consumo da Madeira: https://www.madeira.gov.pt/cacc
  - Centro de Arbitragem da Universidade Autónoma de Lisboa: national; http://arbitragem.autonoma.pt/
  - Specific competence (motor vehicles, insurance, travel agencies) does not concern this shop.
  - The list is undated and may be out of date: for CIMAAL it gives `www.consumidoronline.pt` while its 2026 regulation is published on `consumoalgarve.pt`. Every name and address is checked live before it appears on the public site.
- Art. 18.º n.º 2: the information goes on the website and also in written contracts or another durable medium. For this shop: the terms of sale and the order confirmation email (a Shopify notification template).
- **Open with the client:** whether to adhere to a centre (voluntary); the text of the notice, in the terms of sale and in the footer or a page of its own.

## 8. EU online dispute resolution platform

The European ODR platform was discontinued. Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 repeals Regulation (EU) 524/2013 with effect from 20 July 2025, and the Commission closed the platform; online sellers no longer have to link to it. Many templates still do, and the site must not.

Sources: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3228/oj/eng and https://consumer-redress.ec.europa.eu/site-relocation_en (2026-10-08).

## What the site needs from the client

Before the marketing and legal spec starts, the client provides:

- company name, registered address, commercial registry number, tax number;
- a telephone number and a physical address where complaints can be made (required, art. 4.º n.º 1 a) of DL 24/2014);
- whether the client adheres to a consumer arbitration centre (voluntary; the list of centres is shown either way);
- the decision on the physical Complaints Book;
- the registration of the business on the Complaints Book platform, and whether the footer shows the official icon or the text link.
